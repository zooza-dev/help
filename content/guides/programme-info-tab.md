# Tell enrolled clients the rules of your course

Every provider has the same set of questions arriving by email all term: what should she
bring, which door do we use, can I park there, who do I tell if we cannot come. Since
late October 2026 there is a place to answer them once — a tab on the programme screen
in the **Client Profile**, visible to clients who are enrolled.

It holds whatever you want: what to bring, where to go, house rules, who to contact,
your own FAQ.

> **Who sees it:** enrolled clients, in their Client Profile, on the screen for that
> programme. It does **not** appear on the booking form or in emails, so it is for people
> who have already joined — not a sales page.

---

## Set it up

Go to **Settings → General** and find the **Programme info tab** card.

1. **Tab name.** Pick one of the ready-made names — **Contacts**, **Information**,
   **Instructions** — or type your own. This is the word the client clicks.
2. **Content.** The body of the tab. Formatted text, so headings and lists are fine.
3. Save.

What you set here is the **Default for all programmes**. Set nothing and clients see no
tab at all, unless an individual programme has its own.

### Different wording for one programme

Most providers need one default and a couple of exceptions — the swimming course needs a
towel and a cap, the camp needs a packing list and a pickup time.

On the programme itself, **Settings → Info tab** shows which of these applies and lets
you change it:

| What the card says | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Uses the company default** | The default above, unchanged |
| **Custom for this programme** | This programme has its own wording |
| **Hidden for this programme** | No tab here, even though a default exists |
| **No info tab for this programme** | Nothing is set anywhere |

The actions are **Create for this programme**, **Customise for this programme** (start
from the default and edit), **Hide for this programme**, **Show again**, and **Use
company default** to throw away the custom version and fall back.

Back in Settings, **Programmes with their own tab** lists every exception with its tab
name and status, so you can see at a glance which programmes have drifted from the
default.

> **Deleting a tab removes it from clients' view.** The confirmation says so plainly;
> there is no draft state.

---

## Worth writing in it

A short list beats a long one — this is read by a parent on a phone, usually the evening
before the first session.

- **What to bring**, in the order they will pack it.
- **Where exactly to go** — not just the address. Which entrance, which floor, where to
  wait.
- **How to tell you they cannot come**, and by when. Point them at the cancel button in
  their own profile rather than asking for an email.
- **Who to contact** and how fast you answer.
- **The two or three rules you repeat most.** Shoes, food, siblings, phones.

What not to put here: anything about money or dates that is already on the booking. Those
screens are generated from the real data and will always be right; a paragraph you typed
in March will not be.

## If you run a network

A network HQ can write this content once and have it appear in every unit, which is the
reason the feature exists in this shape.

- **One network-wide tab**, or **one per product code** — so a franchise can give its
  Baby course one set of instructions and its Toddler course another, across every branch.
- Each piece of content is either **enforced** or **customisable**. Enforced means the
  unit can read it but not change it: *"Your network sets the programme info tab for all
  your programmes. You can view it here but not change it."*
- A customisable one can be edited locally, and then shows as **From your network,
  customised**.
- **Reset to network version** replaces your wording with the network's latest.
- **Restore previous** brings back what you had before the network replaced it. Content a
  unit wrote is kept, not destroyed — which is the point of having both buttons.

**While a network-wide tab is enforced**, a unit's own programme tabs are kept but not
shown: *"Your network's info tab is enforced for all programmes. These programme tabs are
kept but are not shown to clients while it is enforced."* Nothing is lost; turn the
enforcement off and they come back.

> **One language per network.** There are no language variants, so a network spanning
> markets needs to decide which language this content is in — or leave it customisable
> and let each unit write its own.
