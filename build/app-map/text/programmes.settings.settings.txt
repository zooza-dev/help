Baker-Miller Pink

In the 1970s, a wave of drug epidemics swept through America, bringing with it increased levels of aggression and violence. Two officers at the Seattle Correctional Facility - Gene Baker and Ron Miller - dealt with this problem on a daily basis. In desperation, they decided on March 1, 1979, based on Professor Schaus' research up to that time, to try painting the holding cells pink. The result was 156 days without incident. Yes, pink reduces aggression, and that includes men.
